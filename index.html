Field Manual

Monsters Manual — what walks the road

  • Walkers and what they spit
  • SHAMBLERReskinned (P26) — individual shambling figures now: hunched backs, tattered rot-green coats, arms reaching, heads lolling. The standard walker. Walks down the road toward your squad and drifts toward your lane. Trades one-for-one on contact — soften it with gunfire first.
  • PUKEWalker attack, tuned up. When a walker cluster gets within ~185px ahead of you and roughly in your lane, it spits an aimed puke blob at the spot you were standing in — the wind-up is short now (first spit ~0.3–0.7s after it enters range) and it refires every ~1.1–1.7s per cluster. A hit costs 1 (armor soaks it first). It is aimed, not homing — sidestep after it spits. But a miss isn't free anymore: see ACID. Walkers also walk a little faster than they used to.
  • ACIDNew. Puke that misses splashes into an acid pool on the road that lasts ~3.6s. Standing in a pool ticks 1 damage every ~0.55s (armor soaks first, and the losses count toward your K/D like any other). Dodging the spit is step one — don't then stand in what it left behind.
  • Speed, bulk, and the mob
  • RUNNERYellow — caution. Fast, lean, low health — and it charges. Runners close the gap at ~1.6x road speed and home in on you. When a runner cluster gets close it detonates — and the read is unmistakable now (P26): the lead runner's chest swells with a pulsing orange core while the pack flashes, on a short fuse, then a splash explosion that costs 3 (armor first) if you're caught in the blast, and also damages nearby zombies. Kill runners at range; if one is already flashing, move — your new up/down movement is the dodge. They remain the direct counter to sitting back and letting a Medic rebuild.
  • BRUTERed. Reskinned (P26) — at his scale he finally reads: slab torso, torn shirt remnant, scar seams, a heavy jaw, knuckles dragging. The tank. One huge zombie with a big health pool, drawn larger on the road. Slow — until it isn't: a brute a stretch of road ahead telegraphs with a pulsing red ring, crouches for a beat, then charges at ~3.6× speed, homing hard on your lane. Sidestep the charge or kill it during the wind-up. The charge is only the first pass (P37): where it ends, the brute winds up again — a red ring swelling to the slam's radius — and ground-slams: 2 damage (armor first) to anyone caught inside the ring when it lands. Then it rests, marked WINDED, barely moving for a beat and a half — that rest is your punish window — before the whole cycle re-arms: charge, slam, rest, repeat. Contact costs 2 per tick (armor first). Normal bullets chip it — you want a Sniper or Grenadier for these.
  • MOB WAVESome gates are guarded: a zombie mob — now often a Brute in the middle with Runners on the flanks — camps the good gate's side and advances toward you. Shoot a hole through it, tank through it, or take the open side and pay the red gate.
  • The boss at the end of the road
  • SUMMONERPurple — a tall hooded figure in a tattered, root-hemmed robe, horned hood over a shadowed face, a crystal-topped staff in hand and the horde orbiting him as visible motes. End-of-run boss, in two stages. Stage one — off-screen: the Summoner stays hidden and untouchable while it calls the horde in order — weakest first (walker swarms), then runners, then brutes last — and lobs acid volleys down the road at you between summons (dodge them; the misses pool, same as walker puke). Stage two — on the field: once its last wave is sent, it comes down the road itself, health = boss-size setting × 3, contact 4 per tick, no melee trade — only firepower grades your build. The acid never stops (P28): the aimed volleys continue through every phase, for his whole life. At half health it roars: screen shake, a much faster press, a second summoned wave, and a final brute charge. Death throes (P28): spending his HP does not end it — he locks onto the squad's position (a red blast circle paints the last place standing), charges that exact spot and explodes for heavy damage inside the radius. Move after the lock and the blast whiffs; the win registers as he detonates.
  • Zone 2 — what roots in the streets
  • MUTANTThe city's new shape — and a hint of the end of the road. A plant-thing in the Rooted's language that still wears the person it was: a head, shoulders, a ribbed torso, two arms — and legs that end in roots. It never moves, rooted to its patch of street with a deep health pool (~60 and up, growing down the road), and knockback simply does not move it. Its right arm has grown long into a tendril whip — a 0.6s wind-up paints the strike lane RED where your squad stands as the arm raises (P28: every telegraphed attack zone, for every mob and boss, renders red — the standard combat color code; projectiles keep their own colors, and an indicator always expires with its attack) — then it lashes for 2 (armor first) onto that lane. The lash lands on the lane, not on you: step out during the wind-up and it whiffs. Contact costs 2 per tick. Kill it at range, or walk the lane dance. It is planted, not rolled: Level 5 "The Streets" introduces it at set points down the road — you will meet it — and it keeps rooting across Zone 2's later stretches, in Level 6's waves and even answering the Torch's calls. In Zone 3 it recurs as a mini-boss — in late waves and guarding the stranded convoy — the same rooted figure, recycled into the rotation. Its plate is worth carrying home: a fallen mutant pays Salvage.
  • The boss of the streets
  • THE TORCHZone 2's boss — a human, and the worst kind: an organized one. Fully redrawn (P26) to stand beside the Mutant: riveted fuel tank and hose, armored rust vest, belt molotovs, goggles pushed up on a scarred, grinning face. Health = boss-size setting × 3 × 1.35 — tougher than the Summoner — and he takes the field directly, pressing down the road, contact 3 per tick. His kit is the raiders', grown up: a flamer on a telegraphed cycle (the wind-up draws his aim line; the flame sweeps toward you — real flame now (P27), layered flickering tongues instead of the old cone, same colors, same coverage — slower than you can run, ticking 1 damage every ~0.4s on whoever stands in it), molotovs tossed two at a time that land as fire patches, and goon summons — raider clusters called in waves, junkies and all. He is also the first boss to summon carrion bats: on his own timer he calls them down over the street — the bats' arrival in Zone 2's second half starts with him. At half health he burns hotter: every cycle runs ~30% faster.
  • What flies — two flyers, two territories
  • CARRION BIRDThe green lands' flyer — Zone 1 is hers. A feathered carrion bird — smooth pointed wings, a beak, a fanned tail — crossing the whole board on a zigzag line at weird angles, never homing, never slowing, gone once she clears the far side. There is no leading her from behind cover: you shoot her on her crossing line, while she is there, or she is past you. Next to nothing for health (4, scaling with the road's pressure like everything else) — pure pressure, nothing more. Contact costs 1. She owns all of Zone 1 and the first half of Zone 2. And she flies on a schedule: flyer passes run on a guaranteed timer in every level from Level 1 on — the first pass seconds into the run, then on a long beat that tightens slightly with level and squad power — not a dice roll. Thinned out (P37): the passes come notably less often than they used to, and swarms are rarer still — the flyers are specials now, spice over the grind, not weather. From Level 4 up, a pass sometimes comes as a swarm instead: a whole flock on staggered crossing lines at once.
  • CARRION BATThe desert flyer — and the desert is bat country. Same crossing-line flight, same tissue-paper health (4, scaling), same 1 contact — told apart from the bird by the silhouette: scalloped wing membranes and ears, no beak, near-black against the sky. The bats enter in the second half of Zone 2, as the city's sky changes — and the Torch is the first boss to summon them, calling them down over his streets. Zone 3 is all bats: the whole desert sky on the same guaranteed pass timer, swarms at higher levels, the convoy detour's second wave, and the Rooted's first-stage calls. The road crews have a name for the desert stretch, borrowed from an old book and not meant as a compliment: bat country. Do not stop there.
  • The end of the road
  • THE ROOTEDZone 3's boss — what the labs made, rooted where it stands. Fully redrawn (P27) — layered bark plates, a root skirt gripping the road, spore pods, a branch crown, and a face in the bark. Stationary at the top of the road; health = boss-size setting × 3 × 1.85, the deepest pool on the road. Combat force-evolves it through three stages, and the stages are gated: crossing a health threshold starts a transformation pause — a pulsing violet seal, the shell shut, it takes no damage and fires nothing — so burst fire cannot skip a stage, and every stage takes the field and fights. Stage 1 — the Tank (down to 66% health): tendril whip lashes on the mutant's telegraph rules (0.6s lane painted red, 2 damage, armor first) and it calls carrion bats. The whips reach the full depth of your movement frame — the far back of the screen is not safe anymore. Stage 2 — the Arms: five tentacle arms rise as separate targets, 42 health each — the original three, and two extra arms (P27) grown into the gaps between them — each lashing on its own cycle (1 damage); while any arm lives, the core takes only ~a third of every hit — break the arms to expose it. Stage 3 — the Core Exposed (arms broken, or health under 30%): the shell opens and it fills the air — aimed 5-way spore spreads (widened and quickened, P27) and 14-shot radial rings every ~2.2s, all single dodgeable dots at an enraged pace. Everything it fires — tendril blasts and spores alike — burns purple, against your green. Destroying the core wins the run — and the zone.

Unit Codex — the squad, by role

  • Command
  • COMMANDERThe Depot's hero unit (bought with Coin, in The Depot). A starred, gold-trimmed figure at the front of the formation carrying the Milemarker — his signature rail-lance. It fires a visible piercing beam (a long gold line, not a dot): heavy damage, a slow rate of fire, and heavy pushback that shoves even the horde back up the road, punching through everything in its line. His Commander's Edge upgrades in the Depot raise the Milemarker's damage. He takes combat hits first: 20 HP stand between the road and your squad, and while he stands he is the one rams, contact, puke and raider fire chew through. Down is down for the rest of the run — unless the Depot sold him a Second Wind. Command Presence (the squad fires 10% faster while he stands) is his other upgrade. Field purchases — Depot Plating (+6 starting armor) and a Medic on Staff — apply every run, and every purchase dresses the detour depot up a little more.
  • Front line — first contact
  • GUNNERSee GUNNER / SHOTGUN below — the Gunner now fires its own 3-round bursts from the front line.
  • GUNNER / SHOTGUNGunner now has its own attack: a 3-round burst (orange tracers, “BURST ×3”) from the front line on its own timer — it no longer just adds a stream. Shotgunner is the spread type: a 5-pellet cone blast at a fixed rate (~1.7s), short range, capped at 3 like every special.
  • FORMATIONThe militia, made over (P27) — full figures now: legs, shaded vests, faces under faction-blue helmets. The reads are unchanged: Gunners front (yellow helmet stripe, raised gun), regulars middle (blue, tiny flag mark), Grenadiers with orange sash, Shotgunners with brown band + wide barrel, Snipers rear (dark cap, long rifle), Medics rear (white + red cross). Skin tones vary per soldier (P28) — every figure draws its tone from a varied palette at spawn, yellows, browns, reds and the tones between; the raiders draw from the same palette. People are people, in every tone, on both sides of the road. A soldier revived by a Medic draws white — and if you have a Medic, the last soldier reads white as the respawn signifier.
  • WEAPONSRoles hit differently now, with pushback: regular fire is standard (small pushback). Gunner bursts are normal rounds. Shotgunner pellets now deal 2 damage each with heavy pushback — it shoves the horde back up the road. Sniper rounds deal 6 with the heaviest pushback. Medics carry machine pistols: low range (210), wide spread, fast fire, low damage (1) — but each hit pushes back, so a medic holds its own ground between the Gunner and the Shotgunner.
  • Support — keeps the line standing
  • MEDICStill in the pool — patches losses back one at a time, up to your squad's peak strength this run. Powerful if you can buy time. Runners and Brutes are built to deny you that time.
  • DMG / ARMORDMG+ makes every bullet hit harder; ARMOR soaks combat losses — rams, zombies, puke, the boss — before they cost troops, though gates always charge full price.
  • Specialists — reach and blast
  • SNIPERNew soldier type. Every ~2.8s per Sniper, fires a piercing 6-damage shot straight up the road. It punches through everything in its line — built for Brutes.
  • GRENADIEREvery ~3.2s per Grenadier, visibly lobs a grenade in an arc at the nearest zombie / barrel ahead — watch for the orange explosion flash and ring: 4 damage in a 72px blast. Best against packed mobs and Brutes standing in a crowd. Like all your fire, it only targets what you can see on screen.
  • The squad ledger
  • ROSTER FIREFixed — fire comes only from living roster types. The squad is tracked soldier by soldier. Base streams scale with regulars actually alive (shown in the loadout line); if you're down to one soldier and that soldier is a Gunner, the only fire from your team is the Gunner's burst — no phantom regular shots. Casualties are taken regulars first, rear line (Snipers, Medics) last.
  • POWERGates now also roll PWR +2/+3 (green) and PWR −2/−3 (red). Power is shown in the loadout line; at PWR 5+ your bullets gain +1 damage, at −5 they lose bite. Special gates rotate: a capped type rolls the next uncapped special instead.
  • RESERVESWhen a special would exceed the 3-of-a-type cap, the overflow becomes Reserves (sideline troops, shown as RES at the top of the screen). Spend them: [1] Airstrike — 3 reserves, heavy damage to the densest on-screen cluster. [2] Gunship escort — 4 reserves, ~6s of extra fire. [3] Deploy — 1 reserve per soldier, brought into the squad on the spot. Buttons work too.
  • UNIT UPGRADESPer-soldier upgrades live in The Depot, bought with Coin. Front-Line Plating fits extra armor for the Gunners and Shotgunners who hold the front — bought plating rolls out as starting armor every run. Double Tap gives each Sniper shot a chance to fire a second heavy round immediately. Medic Field Kit makes Medics revive faster, with a chance to bring back two soldiers on a tick. Grenadier Blast Kit widens the grenade blast, and at level 2 adds damage. Each unit's sheet grows in the Depot as the shelf does.

Other Factions — the other people out there

  • Raiders — the other humans on the road
  • RAIDERSThe other humans on the road — now armed, kit-bashed. Some detours are not zombie holdouts: raiders — their own rust-orange figures now (P27): torn vests, headbands and hoods, the cluster's weapon carried in hand — hold captured soldiers at the depot and fight in waves with the same structure as the horde. Nothing they carry is standard issue: each cluster rolls one weapon, labeled on the road — a hunting rifle (slow, longest reach), a shotgun (short range, 3-pellet spread) or revolvers (medium rate). Their fire is crude on purpose: slow, 1 damage a hit, aimed at where you stood — sidestep and it misses. Lately their fire has picked up a notch — the reset between shots is shorter than it was. The trade runs the other way too: raiders carry loot now, and a fallen cluster can drop Coin and Supplies on the road. Clear all three waves and the captives split — part join the squad immediately (troop size up), part bank as Reserves.
  • SWIPE / MOLOTOVRaiders are melee people with guns. Get within reach and the whole cluster swipes — 1 damage on a slow cooldown, flashed as an arc. At range they lob Molotovs — and they throw them far more often than they used to (P28): a lit bottle arcs to where you stood and leaves a fire patch for ~3s that ticks 1 damage every ~0.55s while you stand in it (armor first) — the raider echo of the walkers' acid pools. Don't stand in the fire.
  • JUNKIEThe raider answer to your Medic. Some clusters travel with a junkie — a single marked figure in lime with a pulsing aura ring. While it lives, raiders near it are buffed: they shoot faster, a buffed rifle hits for 2, their swipes hit for 2, and heavy rounds lose a point of damage against them. Buffed clusters wear an orange aura tint and read BUFFED on the road — shoot the junkie first and the tint, and the buff, goes away.
  • Civilians — why you run the road
  • CIVILIANSUnarmed people caught on the road — tan figures, no weapons, waiting at the depot or packed into a barrel. Rescued civilians become Reserves: the sideline troops that power Airstrike, Gunship escort and Deploy. A civilian detour pays +6; a barrel rescue pays +2.

Controls — hands on the road

  • STEERDrag on the road, or ←→↑↓ / WASD. Diagonals work — you have a small up/down frame on top of left/right: enough to dodge puke, pools and charges, not enough to leave the fight.
  • AIMThree modes, buttons above the road: Forward fires straight up-road, always (best for shooting gates) — and the hardest mode: nothing tracks for you. Directional follows your mouse pointer — fire angles from the squad toward wherever you point (on touch it slants with your steering lean). Locked auto-tracks the nearest visible target, sides included — shooting gates becomes a choice, not a default.
  • SKILLS1 Airstrike — 3 Reserves, flattens the densest cluster. 2 Gunship escort — 4 Reserves, ~6s of fire support. 3 Deploy — 1 Reserve per soldier, sideline troops join on the spot. The circular buttons docked along the bottom of the game screen do the same — tap a circle mid-fight, no scrolling: each carries its symbol and its live use-count, and dims when you can't afford it. The RES readout at the top of the screen shows the Reserves total, and the dock circles carry the per-skill counts: with 12 Reserves they read Airstrike ×4, Gunship ×3, Deploy ×12.
  • PAUSESpace or the Pause button. The pause menu carries Resume, Restart, Main Menu and Settings — Main Menu ends the run, recorded as a defeat at your current standing, and returns to the cover. The run timer freezes while paused — and while Settings or the Field Manual are open. Opening the manual or settings also fully suspends steering: the click that opens the book can never drag the squad.
  • MENUSThe ⚙ Settings button (corner of the game screen) holds the tuning sliders. The Field Manual button holds this book. The game screen itself keeps only the play field, the HUD/score, aim, Reserves and skills.

How a Run Works — the escort's job

  • The job
  • OPENINGThe first 30 seconds teach the loop. Every run opens with a barrel, then a gate, in the first few seconds — the game's two core choices (shoot the gamble, pick a side) before the stages start teaching the rest.
  • THE ZONESThree zones, nine levels, unlocked in order. Every level is the full road. Clearing a zone pays 100 / 150 / 250 Coin.
    L1 First Mile — shamblers only, gates & barrels, no boss; reach the end.
    L2 The Noise Grows — runners, brutes, puke/acid, mob waves, one ramp.
    L3 The Summoner — full run + boss; clears Zone 1. Birds overhead throughout Zone 1.
    L4 The Tree Line — city approach; raiders common, birds still own the sky.
    L5 The Streets — the Mutant arrives; halfway down, bats take the sky.
    L6 The Torch — raider boss, flamer & molotovs, first bat summons; clears Zone 2.
    L7 The Long Sand — desert, bat country, mutants in the sand.
    L8 The Approach — everything Zone 3 has, thicker.
    L9 The Rooted — final boss in three stages; clears Zone 3.
  • LOREThe road is the last supply line and the squad is its escort. Win and the road stays open one more day because of what you carried through; lose and it goes dark — the end screen keeps its practical tips, framed in that world.
  • Gates, barrels, and the gamble
  • +12Green gates add to your squad. Run through the side you want — you only get one side of each gate.
  • ×2Multipliers are the big swings. A ×2 at 40 is worth more than three small gates — if you reach it alive.
  • −8Red gates cut your numbers, and ÷2 is the worst of them. Sometimes both sides are bad and it's about losing less — but never twice in a row, and the gate after a both-red pair always carries a strong upgrade.
  • SCALE-LINKEDGates are linked to the squad they meet (P26, rebalanced P28). An additive gate pays its base plus 7% of your current squad (armor gates scale the same way) — never less than the base, so the early game feels unchanged. A flat red gate costs its base plus 14% of your current squad — negatives link heavier than positives now, so a red gate always takes a serious chunk, even at snowball scale. PWR gates get a light link (+1 per 90 squad, up to +3). × and ÷2 gates were already proportional and do not change. Gate labels show the linked value you will actually pay or gain. And the squad has a hard ceiling of 2,000 (P28): overflow from gates, barrels and rescues banks into Reserves in the campaign — and in Into the Dust it converts straight to score instead. Nothing grows without bound anymore.
  • GATE MATHShoot the gates — within limits (P28). Numeric gates are live math, but shooting is capped now. Every 4 bullet hits on a side raises its number — but a negative gate can only be shot up to zero: neutralized, never flipped positive (the old −900-shot-to-+1200 road is closed). A positive gate keeps a small bump per 4 hits, with a hard cap of 3 total bumps per side; after that, fire does nothing to it. A division gate hit enough times softens into a plain negative, then climbs to zero the same way; a ×2 can inch toward ×3 only with heavy fire. Power gates obey the same law: a PWR −2 can be shot to 0 and no further, and a positive PWR takes at most two bumps. Special gates (DMG, ARMOR, MEDIC, soldier types) don't take math — they stay as rolled, and a specialist rolled past the 3-of-a-type cap banks into Reserves instead. You have to choose: spend your fire on the gate's number, or on the zombies in front of it.
  • BARRELBarrels have hit points — the number on the lid. Shoot it open before you reach it: most hold recruits, some hold a random upgrade (damage, armor, a medic, a soldier type…), a smaller share are a zombie ambush, some hold civilians who join the sideline as +2 Reserves, and some spill Coin straight into your bank — the gamble is the point. Ram it and you lose people instead.
  • SPACINGGate and event spacing has more RNG now — mostly the familiar rhythm, with occasional tight clusters and occasional long, quieter stretches before the next gate. Don't assume the next gate is a fixed distance away.
  • Moving, and the road's answer
  • MOVENew — a small movement frame. You can now move up and down as well as left and right — drag in any direction, or use arrow keys / WASD. Diagonals work. The frame is deliberately small: enough to dodge puke and acid, line up a gate, back off a charge, or close in to finish a barrel — not enough to leave the fight.
  • SCALINGZombie spawns scale slightly with your squad's power — headcount, damage, streams, and soldier types all count. A stronger build draws a slightly stronger pushback, so snowballing never makes the road free. The pressure is general now: spawn density and wave size ride the same scale — the better you do, the more the world throws at you, bounded as always. What does not scale: the Summoner's pacing, the acid volleys, the barrel ambush rate. Those cadences are the road's manners.
  • STAGESNew structure. The road now runs in stages, announced on screen as you cross into each: Stage 1 — Swarm (walkers, with their faster spit), Stage 2 — Runners (speed and herding), Stage 3 — Tanks (brutes, charging). The current stage shows in the loadout line under the progress bar. Survive all three and the Summoner's gate opens.
  • RANGERoot fix in P9. Every bullet now has a hard max range (400px, shotgun 240) shorter than the visible field and fades out in its last 70px — you can see fire end. The Summoner takes no damage until fully entered (y > 86, shield ring shown while entering). Runners that pass the squad and leave the field are removed silently — no off-screen explosions.
  • COLORSRead the road by color: soldiers blue, walkers sickly green, runners yellow (caution), brutes red, and the Summoner purple.
  • How the run is scored
  • SCOREEvery run is scored, itemized on the end screen. Zombies down ×10 · soldiers brought home ×25 · civilians rescued ×5 — civilians rescued during the run: the people you pull out on exit-ramp detours, plus civilians found packed in barrels · waves faced ×100 · detours cleared ×250 · distance covered ×10 per percent · victory bonus +1,000. Bringing people home pays on the same scale as raw kills — a clean run beats a bloody one. Make the top 10 and the game asks for your three initials, arcade-style; the table lives in the Scores tab. Reserves left over also convert to Coin (1 = 5) for the Depot shop.
  • WAVESWhat "Waves faced" counts: the mob waves on the main road — the guarded-gate mobs (a brute with runner flanks) that camp a gate and advance on you. Detour waves and the Summoner's called waves are not counted in that number.
  • K / DLive in the HUD and on the end screen. Kills ÷ soldiers lost, counted honestly: every troop lost counts — gates, rams, zombie contact, puke, acid, and the boss — and a Medic patching someone back does not un-count the loss. Lose nobody and it reads FLAWLESS — kills with 0 lost — instead of an infinity symbol. A win with a bad K/D and a win with a clean one are different runs; this is the number that shows the tactical choices, not just the result.
  • DISTANCEFixed, and ramps count. End-screen distance measures the whole run — road plus the Summoner fight — not just the road, and distance covered on exit-ramp detours counts too: the meters you run on a ramp are added to the journey and to the total. The road alone fills in about 12 seconds, which is why an early death used to read ~98%; dying at ~12 seconds now reads roughly 40%, and only an actual win reads 100%.
  • TIMERThe clock in the HUD runs from the first frame of the run to the Summoner falling or the squad falling, pauses when you pause, and is shown again on the end screen. Prototype 6 boss runs on Normal landed 0:23–0:28 — the Summoner's two stages will stretch that again; new averages still welcome.
  • Past the campaign
  • THE ENDINGWhen the Rooted falls, the story gets its moment before the paperwork. A short cutscene plays over the road at rest — the zones passed, the compounds waking, the road opening — line by line. Tap, click or press Skip to go straight to the closing card: THE ROAD IS OPEN, with the run's totals.
  • INTO THE DUSTEndless Mode — Into the Dust, entered from The Zone. Choose a zone — 1, 2 or 3 — and its road runs endlessly through that zone's land, enemy set and sky. One leg is the standard 5,100; pressure scales with distance and compounds leg by leg (×1.22 per leg on spawn density and enemy health, and enemy contact damage grows with the legs), and at each leg's end the zone's boss returns as a gate — beat it to keep rolling, and it comes back compounding ×1.55 stronger every appearance. The design target is a wall: the snowball always meets it, and every Into the Dust run ends. Exit ramps still appear. It is a solo challenge: nothing carries in or out — no Commander, no inventory, no purchased upgrades, no building bonuses, and no Coin, Supplies or Salvage paid to the campaign. Your tuning sliders still apply, because the tuning table is the player's. A wipe ends the run. Its score — distance first, then kills, waves and detours — lives on its own per-zone boards in the Scores tab.

World & Events — the road itself

  • The world you feed
  • COMPOUNDSWhy the road exists. Scattered along the highway sit the compounds — vault-like settlements, sealed and stubborn, that survived by walling themselves in. Compounds trade: food, medicine, parts, news — and trade needs the road. By old-world policy the highways were built for trade and military movement only, and that policy outlived the world that wrote it. No road, no trade; no trade, no compounds. Your squad is the escort that keeps the policy true — every run is a delivery the whole chain is waiting on.
  • BUNKER BRAINSTrained boots on the ground, brains in the bunkers. The compound's smartest people never take the road — they run the complicated work from underground: the airstrike calls, the gunship escort, the reinforcement queues behind your Reserves. When you spend Reserves, a bunker mind is spending them with you — the skills are not magic, they are the best people the compound has, working a board you never see.
  • SUPPLIESThe second purse. Coin buys steel; Supplies buy time. Every won battle pays Supplies, every detour stocks them alongside the rescue — the holdout shares its stores, the raider camp gets looted — and raider clusters drop them when they fall. Spend them in the Depot on the Farm: tiers that bank Reserves before a run starts and fatten every rescue's yield. Supplies show on the cover and the end screen, next to the Coin.
  • ZONE 1 — GREEN LANDSZone 1 is the green lands — fairly normal country, not much to see, and that is the point: this is where the farms and the safety are, the ground the compounds eat from. The horde here is the spillover, not the source. Hold the Highway and the green lands hold; the Farm in the Depot is this zone working for you between runs. Overhead, carrion birds work this sky — they cross the road on their zigzag lines in every level here, on a regular pass you can set your watch by.
  • ZONE 2 — THE CITYBuilt — Levels 4–6, unlocked when Zone 1 is cleared. When the fall came, humans left the cities for the safety of the wilds — only the crazy stayed, and the craziest of them run the streets now. The road scars heavier here: dead trees, ruined buildings, cracked grey asphalt. Raiders are drastically more common — they ride the road itself, not just the camps. Zone 2 is the raiders' arc: their boss, the Torch, takes the field himself, a flamer in hand, molotovs at his belt, summoning his goons the way the Summoner calls the horde. And something new roots in his streets — the mutant: a hint of what waits further out, wearing a shape that used to be a person. The sky changes halfway through the zone: carrion birds own the first half, then the crossings go dark and scalloped — carrion bats have arrived, and the Torch, the zone's boss, is the first to summon them. The exit ramps here lead to a barricaded shelter in a ruined building, pressed by raider-heavy waves.
  • ZONE 3 — GROUND ZEROBuilt — Levels 7–9, unlocked when Zone 2 is cleared. Past the city the land gives out to desert — cactus and rocks at the verges, the road at its cracked and blasted worst — and the desert runs to the labs at ground zero. The road crews have a name for this whole stretch, borrowed from an old book about fear on a desert highway: bat country — we can't stop here. Carrion bats own the entire sky now, zigzagging across the board on retro arcade lines; mutants recur as mini-bosses, in late waves and rooted beside the exit ramps' stranded convoy. The boss there is the Rooted — a human mutant less human and more stationary, force-evolved through its stages by your fire: the tank, the arms, the exposed core filling the air with dense patterns — not a full bullet hell, but close enough to respect.
  • On the road itself
  • EXIT RAMPThe rescue detour — up to two per run, on either side. Every run rolls its ramp plan: one ramp near the midpoint, or two — one in each half — never back to back. Each offer now rolls its side: the ramp can appear on the left or the right, and the on-screen instruction tells you which way to steer. Steer to that side as the sign reaches you to take it; each offer is once-only, and ignoring one changes nothing. On the detour, main-road distance pauses: a short pass, 3 waves, no boss, ending at a depot. The motif is the zone's: Zone 1 is the farm holdout (or a raider camp), Zone 2 a barricaded shelter in a ruined building with raider-heavy waves, Zone 3 a stranded convoy off-road — the ramp leaves the asphalt and the detour runs on a dirt track, tire ruts and dust, no paint — zombie, bat and mutant mixes, a mutant rooted by the trucks in the last wave. A civilian rescue pays +6 Reserves and +5 Supplies; a raider camp pays freed soldiers instead — part join the squad on the spot, part bank as Reserves — plus +3 Supplies of loot. Wipe on the detour and the run ends there. Detour ground counts toward your distance.
  • STAGESThe road runs in three stages, announced as you cross into each: Stage 1 — Swarm (walkers and their spit), Stage 2 — Runners (speed, herding, detonations), Stage 3 — Tanks (brutes and their charges). The bigger and louder your squad gets, the more the road answers: spawn counts, brute health and the mix all curb gently upward with your squad's power — more soldiers make more noise, and noise attracts hordes. Survive all three stages and the Summoner's gate opens.
  • SUMMONERThe finale, in phases. Phase one — off-screen: untouchable, it calls the horde weakest-first to strongest-last and lobs acid volleys between summons; the misses pool on the road. Phase two — on the field: it takes the road itself (health = boss-size setting × 3, contact 4 per tick, no melee trade) while escort packs keep arriving, and the acid volleys keep coming the whole time. At half health it roars — faster press, a second summoned wave, and a brute on a final charge. And when his HP is spent he gets one last-ditch charge: he locks onto where the squad stands, rushes that spot and detonates — step out of the red circle after the lock and it costs you nothing. It takes no damage until it has fully entered the field.
  • GATESEvery gate is a choice of one side — and live math, capped (P28). Shoot a numeric or power gate to raise it: every 4 hits bumps it — but a red gate can only be shot up to zero, never positive, and a green gate takes at most 3 bumps total. Division softens into a plain negative first; ×2 inches toward ×3 only under heavy fire. Positives pay base +7% of your squad; flat negatives charge base +14%. Special gates (DMG, armor, medic, soldier types) stay as rolled, and a specialist taken at the 3-of-a-type cap banks into Reserves instead. Gate spacing rolls with wide variance — tight clusters and long quiet stretches both happen.
  • BARRELThe road's gamble. Barrels have hit points — the number on the lid. Shoot one open before you reach it: most hold recruits, some a random upgrade, some civilians (+2 Reserves), a smaller share are a zombie ambush — and some now spill Coin straight into your bank. Ram one instead and you lose people. What a barrel holds is never marked; cracking it is always a decision.
  • ACID / FIREGround effects punish standing still. Missed walker puke and the Summoner's volleys splash into acid pools (~3.6s, 1 damage every ~0.55s stood in, armor first). Raider Molotovs leave fire patches on the same rules. Dodge the shot, then don't stand in what it left behind — your up/down movement frame is the way out.

Glossary — The Depot & the Commander

Reference only — the buying happens in The Depot, on the cover. This is the shop, compound-style: Reserves left at the end of a run convert to Coin (5 each, more with Deep Pockets), and barrels sometimes spill Coin directly — bring people home, bank the sideline, spend it there. Purchases apply from your next run. The shelves hang on Haven: the Commander's Tent carries the Commander line, the Depot building carries storage and field lines (and pays +15% Coin storage on every run once it stands), the Armory carries the per-unit lines, and the Farm tiers need the Farm plot staked. Raise the buildings from the cover's Haven button — anyone who already owns purchases from a shelf counts as having that building. Three purses now: Coin buys steel, Supplies buy time, Salvage ranks the Commander's loot. Coin and purchases are saved in this browser's local storage: the file carries the system, and each machine/browser keeps its own bank and depot.

  • The Commander — his kit
  • ★ COMMANDERThe Commander — Milemarker. A hero unit joins the formation — a starred, gold-trimmed figure at the front carrying the Milemarker, his signature rail-lance: a visible piercing beam with heavy damage, a slow rate of fire and heavy pushback. He takes combat hits first: 20 HP between the squad and the road. If he falls, he is down for the rest of the run.
  • MILEMARKERHis signature rail-lance. It fires a visible piercing beam (a long gold line, not a dot) that punches through everything in its line. Commander's Edge raises its damage: +2 damage on the Milemarker rail-lance, per level (max 5). Command Presence: while the Commander stands, the whole squad fires 10% faster. Second Wind: once per run, when the Commander falls he gets back up at half health instead.
  • His three slots — loot, tiers, Salvage
  • THREE SLOTSThe road drops loot now — from bosses (always), barrels, detours, events and the mutant's plate. Loot comes in ranked tiers, I to III. The Commander carries three slots (the rule of 3): Coin opens a slot. An item's bonus applies only while it is equipped in a slot — unequipped loot is inactive: it grants nothing, and its rank-up stays locked until it is equipped. Salvage ranks an equipped item up, and anything he does not need recycles back into Salvage. Salvage itself comes from boss kills, cleared detours, raider scrap and recycling — the third purse, beside Coin and Supplies.

  • PLATED RIG+4 starting armor per tier. Kit-bashed plate, welded by someone who cared.
  • HAIR TRIGGER+8% fire rate per tier. Filed down by hand; the bunkers approve.
  • LUCKY CHARMBetter luck per tier: barrels spill Coin more often, and the road drops loot more often.
  • PHOENIX PINThe Commander gets back up once per run when he falls (a second time if Second Wind is also his).
  • SCAVENGER KIT+25% Salvage from every source, per tier. Pockets, hooks, and an eye for what is worth carrying.

High scores — top 10

Every run is scored on the end screen — zombies down ×10 · soldiers brought home ×25 · civilians rescued ×5 (people rescued on exit-ramp detours, plus civilians found in barrels) · waves faced ×100 · detours cleared ×250 · distance ×10 per percent · victory +1,000. "Waves faced" counts the mob waves on the main road — the guarded-gate mobs that advance on you mid-run. It does not count detour waves or the waves the Summoner calls. Crack the top 10 and the game asks for your three initials, arcade-style. The table is saved in this browser's local storage, alongside your Coin bank, Depot purchases and Zone 1 progress.

Zone boards — the campaign, zone by zone

Each zone keeps its own board: that zone's best runs, and a zone total tallied from the zone's level bests — your best score on each of its three levels, added together. The campaign final score is the three zone totals summed. Every run now records the level it was played on; runs scored before the zone boards opened carry no level mark, so they stand on the overall table above only.

Log — the arc of the thing

Milestones only — how the game got its shape, not every prototype's notes. The full build record lives with the makers.

  • EVERY SCREEN SINGSPrototype 41 — a theme for the fights, and a tune for every room. The three zone loops gain company, all synthesized live the same way — no audio files. The boss theme: one driving track for all three boss fights — faster pulse, doubled bass drive, a tightened minor-key arp and a backbeat snare — that takes over while a boss is alive and hands back to the zone loop at the seam when it falls. The cover gets the Zone 1 motif slowed and stripped — a lone square lead over sparse triangle bass — so starting a Zone 1 run feels like the theme standing up. Haven turns warm and homely, triangle-led and major-leaning, the one safe room. The Depot marches: snare-forward cadence, clipped bass, short bugle-like figures. The Zone map thinks in sparse plucked notes and air, and the Field Manual reads to the driest loop of all — a low studious pulse with soft page-ticks. Each screen's music starts on entering and hands off cleanly on leaving; pause and end screens stay silent, and the Sound button still mutes the world. And on the road: the Rooted's bat call is a mob now — four to six bats, split across both screen edges, converging on the squad as distraction and damage soak for the boss. Standard bat health, standard 1 contact — meat shields, not a damage spike. The boss's health, its other summons, prices and sliders are untouched.
  • THE SOUND PASSPrototype 40 — the road learns to sing, quietly. Each zone gets its own chiptune loop, synthesized live in the browser — square-wave arp, triangle bass, noise percussion, no audio files: Zone 1 road-weary and open, Zone 2 tighter, Zone 3 sparse and strange, every loop stating its zone from the first bar. The mix gets modern engineering without losing the chip accent: a master compressor, clean attack/decay envelopes so nothing clicks, stereo placement that matches where a sound happens on the road, and the music ducking briefly under the biggest hits. And the effects turn informative: the Brute's charge and ground-slam wind-ups are audible before they land, a flyer's approach whistles in from the side it enters, each weapon keeps its own voice, boss deaths detonate properly, and banking pays out with a short coin cascade. Restraint throughout — nothing plays that doesn't tell you something. No gameplay, balance or prices change.
  • SPAWN ECOLOGY & THE SLAMPrototype 37 — specials become spice, and the Brute learns a second pass. The ambient rates for the special classes drop hard in every level they appear in: brutes fall to a fraction of their old share of the road mix, the ambient mutant roll thins to a rare bad-RNG rooting (the planted set-piece mutants are untouched), and the flyer cadence stretches out — longer beats, rarer swarms. Shamblers, standard runners, raider clusters and mob waves are exactly as they were: they are the grind now, and the specials are what interrupts it. Exit-ramp detours change shape to match: every detour's final wave is anchored by a special acting as a mini-boss, scaled to the level — a deepened-pool brute at the farm holdout, the shelter and the raider camp; the convoy keeps its rooted mini-mutant. And the Brute's attack becomes a cycle: charge → ground slam → rest → repeat. The slam winds up behind a red ring painted at its true radius and lands for area damage in the contact family; the rest that follows is a genuine punish window, marked WINDED. Boss fights, boss summons and Level 1's shamblers-only road are unchanged, and the tuning sliders remain the difficulty dial.
  • POLISH & RESETPrototype 35 — hard reset, the doll, and colors that mean something. Settings gains a two-step hard reset back to a fresh install. The Commander pane gains a paper-doll loadout — his figure, his three slots where they sit (tier pips showing, sealed slots locked), and a close-up of the Milemarker with its Edge tier. Buttons learn one language: gold spends, green opens & grows, blue converts, grey is maxed. Haven's townies now draw skin tones from the same palette as everyone else — all colors welcome. The manual stacks clean on a phone, chip on top, text full-width.
  • THE DEPOT STANDS ALONEPrototype 34 — the shop leaves the book. The Depot shelves, the Commander and his inventory, and the Armory/Farm shelves are now their own quick-action screen off the cover — name, level, cost, one line, button — built for buying and getting back to the road. Everything they used to explain moved here, into this manual's new Depot & Commander glossary: what the Depot is, the Commander, his kit, the Milemarker, his three slots, and every shelf's flavor, preserved in full. The Field Manual itself becomes the cover's fifth button, below The Zone, in a quiet slate — a reference book, nothing more. And the phone shop bug is fixed: cards stack, info on top and buttons in a row beneath, with room at the bottom so the last card never hides behind the system bars.
  • BALANCE & CAPSPrototype 28 — gates rebalanced, the Summoner's last stand, red telegraphs, and hard caps. Gate scale-links rebalance: positives pay base +7% of the squad, negatives charge base +14% — and gate shooting is capped: a negative gate can be shot only to zero, never positive, and a positive gate takes at most 3 bumps total. The Summoner hurls his aimed acid volleys through every phase now, and when his HP is spent he locks onto the squad's last position, charges it and detonates — avoidable by moving after the lock; the win registers as he blows. Every telegraphed attack zone renders red for all mobs and bosses, and indicators now always expire with their attack (the stuck-decal bug is fixed at the root). Raiders throw molotovs far more often. Soldiers and raiders alike draw varied skin tones per figure at spawn. And the crash ceiling is designed out: the squad caps at 2,000 (overflow banks to Reserves in the campaign, converts to score in Into the Dust), with entity ceilings on enemies, bullets and particles besides.
  • FLAME & FACESPrototype 27 — the flamer burns like flame, and the last casts get their makeover. The Torch's flamer keeps its colors, coverage and damage but renders as layered, flickering tongues of fire — no more cone. The soldiers/militia (faction blue, every specialist still legible at a glance) and the raiders (rust-orange, the junkie still lime-marked) are redrawn at the roster's standard; the zombies stand as they are — simple, cracked and bloody is the doctrine. The Rooted is redrawn too, and grows teeth: Stage 2 rises with five arms, two of them new, and Stage 3's spore patterns run denser — 5-way spreads, 14-shot rings. Haven turns green-teal and The Zone slate violet on the cover, and Endless Mode — Into the Dust gets a proper header: large, letter-spaced and ruled, on its title card at run start and wherever the mode is picked and tallied.
  • THE WALLPrototype 26 — scale-linked gates, a wall for the Dust, and the roster pass. Gates now pay and charge against the squad they meet (base ±10% of current squad; PWR lightly linked; shooting bumps scale too), so no gate goes meaningless at snowball scale. Into the Dust compounds hard — pressure ×1.22 a leg, contact damage growing, boss gates ×1.55 an appearance — every run ends. Shamblers, Runners, the Brute and the Torch are redrawn at the Summoner/Mutant standard, paint only. The pause menu gains Restart, Main Menu and Settings; the cover reorders to Start Run (naming the current selection), Haven, The Depot, The Zone — Home Base becomes Haven, the Zone Map becomes The Zone. And the Field Manual click no longer drags the squad: steering suspends while any overlay is open.

The solo endless mode that first carried that name is renamed in the same pass: Endless Mode — Into the Dust. Names only — no mechanics, numbers or art change.
